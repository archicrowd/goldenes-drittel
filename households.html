Average wage: OECD Taxing Wages 2024 (Germany 63,288 €; France 44,968 €; Italy 35,616 €; Spain 31,698 €; Poland 96,421 zł). Better off: share of employees of this type across the wage distribution, scaled from Germany (assumption).

What drives the differences

  • One-earner couples with higher wages lose part of today's joint taxation (German splitting, French quotient familial): every adult is taxed individually and receives an own dividend. Around twice the average wage the gain shrinks to about zero in Germany and France.
  • France at half the average wage: today's in-work benefit (prime d'activité) and family allowances are replaced by the dividend and the floor; for single people at half the average wage this is a loss of 148 € per month. Employer contributions near the minimum wage are already largely waived today (RGDU), so little is passed on.
  • Italy, Spain and Poland at low wages: today's minimum income schemes reach fewer households; the floor and the dividend raise low incomes more strongly.
  • Legacy surcharge: France (21.3 %) and Italy (22.3 %) carry higher surcharges than Germany (15.0 %) and Poland (13.5 %) until 2058.

Germany: microsimulation and pensioners

A synthetic microsimulation calibrated to official totals finds 85.1 % of working-age households and 69.3 % of all households better off; households whose main earner has a mini-job 49.1 %, households without earnings (Bürgergeld) 18.0 %. Pensioners: 32.2 % better off, mean change −121 € per month, top fifth −478 €. Validation 2025: gross wages −2.8 %, wage tax −5.4 % against official figures.

Germany: the € 42,000 example

Single +611 €, single parent +610 €, couple with one earner and two children +674 € per month (gross wage € 42,000, about two thirds of the average wage). Sensitivity to pass-through, surcharge, saving and fund interest: German page.

Country models are simplified (one earner per household; regional rules, housing benefits beyond those named and behavioural responses not included). The share of employees better off uses the German wage distribution scaled to each country's average wage; national wage distributions are an open task.
