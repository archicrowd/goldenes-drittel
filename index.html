The Golden Third – calculations, reviews and data

A proposal for taxation and social protection shared by a group of states: no euro of income taxed at more than one third, social contributions replaced by a strong safety net without premiums, a monthly transformation dividend for every adult, a funded pension account of one's own, and a common fund for legacy debt and pension promises. These pages are written for research and review: they show the core calculations with their assumptions, the reviews so far, and the figures in citable form.

Draft for discussion, not an adopted programme. Model version v4.59, 1 November 2026. General website in six languages: archicrowd.de · Deutsch

The building blocks

Income tax0 / 11 / 19 / 25 / 33 % in steps tied to each country's basic needs; capital income, profits and inheritance at most 33 %; company profits taxed where customers are, including digital platforms
ContributionsSocial contributions abolished; employers must pass at least 80 % of the contributions that disappear into wages (health, long-term care, pensions, unemployment, family)
Strong netHealth care, long-term care, minimum pension, basic needs in disability and unemployment, financed from taxes (about 9.6 % of GDP in Germany); low earners are topped up on a pre-filled application, own earnings count by half
DividendMonthly payment to every adult, children 80 %, without application; financed from steering taxes and the gains of digitalisation; it shrinks as emissions disappear – by design
Own routeCompulsory saving of 10 % of gross wages into a personal account, locked until retirement age, few investment options and a default fund
VAT label5 / 11 / 17 / 25 %, special rate 33 % only for harmful goods; lower rates through certification by supervised bodies
LegacyPublic debt and unfunded pension promises in a common fund of the participating states: contribution 5.17 % of GDP, debt peak 148.1 % of GDP in 2055, repaid by 2098; existing pensions above basic needs reduced by 15 %

Which states?

The calculations cover a possibility space of 30 European states, with France, Italy, Spain and Poland as reference countries besides Germany. Common rules only work where they are broadly accepted and reliably enforced. A selection by structural criteria – rule of law, democratic legitimacy, data availability, acceptance – rather than by geography is under discussion; on the World Justice Project's Rule of Law Index 2025, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and Japan score at the level of the strongest European states. These states are not calculated in the model; the index is used here only as one possible criterion.

How the figures are produced

All values come from one open chain of calculations (69 stages) based on official statistics (Eurostat, OECD, national statistical offices) and published surveys. The country tax models are checked against the OECD's Taxing Wages 2026: 7 of 10 household types lie within ±2 percentage points of the OECD tax wedge; the deviations for France and Poland are explained. For Germany, a synthetic microsimulation checks the distribution across all households. Every change and every error found is logged with a number (83 corrections so far). The model is static: labour supply, saving and migration do not respond; pass-through, avoidance and take-up enter as assumptions or bands. A qualitative review of behavioural effects has been done; the quantitative stage is open.

The three reviews so far (October 2026) were commissioned by the initiative and cover the model up to version v4.42. Changes up to v4.59 have not yet been reviewed (list). Independent review is explicitly invited; the material is ready.
